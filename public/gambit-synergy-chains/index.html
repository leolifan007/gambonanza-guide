Gambonanza Gambit Combinations - How to Pick Gambits That Work Together

Quick Fix

You can only hold five Gambits, so every slot counts. Pick combinations whose triggers fire on the same action:

Tempo plus captureA capture that also skips the enemy turnYou take a piece and keep the initiative

How to Build a Combination

Rule 1: Start from a trigger, not a name

Every Gambit fires from a condition. Before you buy, ask which of these your board already does every game:

Your board already…Then look for Gambits that trigger on…
Captures pieces oftenCapture triggers (Silver Fork’s, Beth’s)
Lands pieces from StockLanding triggers (Skydiver’s, Spy’s, Dungeon’s)
Pushes pawnsPawn triggers (Squirrel’s, Bug Catcher’s, Race Flag’s)
Fights for tempoTurn-altering effects (Thunder’s)

If a Gambit’s trigger never happens on your board, it is a dead slot, no matter how strong it looks.

Rule 2: Add a second Gambit that fires on the same action

The strongest setups put two Gambits on one trigger. For example, if Thunder’s already makes your pawn captures skip the enemy turn, add a second capture-triggered Gambit so the same capture pays out twice.

Combinations That Actually Work

1. Tempo Plus Capture (Most Reliable)

Gambits: Thunder’s plus a capture-triggered Gambit such as Silver Fork’s.

How it works: a pawn capture takes a piece, skips the enemy turn, and fires your second Gambit on the same capture.

Why it is strong: It does not need rare pieces, so it works in almost any run.

2. Pawn Payout Stack (Economy)

Gambits: Squirrel’s plus Bug Catcher’s plus Race Flag’s.

How it works: pawn moves and pawn captures each trigger a payout, so a pawn-heavy board keeps gold flowing.

Why it is strong: Cheap Gambits that convert into a fast promotion and a steady income. See the Pawn Economy Loop for the full plan.

3. Landing Plus Payout (Tempo Neutral)

Gambits: Skydiver’s or Dungeon’s plus a Gold Tile.

How it works: you spend a turn to land, which you often have to do anyway, and the landing both repositions and pays out.

Why it is strong: It turns a landing turn, which usually costs tempo, into a turn that earns.

4. Queen Support (Late Game)

Gambits: Beth’s plus a recapture-support piece.

How it works: Beth’s pays out around a surviving queen, so you protect the queen and let it carry.

Why it is strong: The queen is the strongest piece, and this combination rewards keeping it alive.

5. Boss-Answer Flex (Always Have the Tool)

Gambits: four for your core direction plus one flexible slot you swap each shop.

How it works: the shop previews the next boss, so you swap the flex slot to whatever answers that modifier.

Why it is strong: Bosses change a rule, so a board with one answer slot loses far fewer games. See the Boss Battle Guide.

Anti-Synergy: Combinations That Fight Themselves

Gambit AGambit BWhy it fails
A capture trigger on a board that rarely capturesAnythingThe first Gambit never fires
Two expensive Gambits on a board that can only trigger oneAnythingOne slot is wasted
A landing trigger with no Gold Tile planAnythingThe landing turn earns nothing extra
A slow payoff with a fast, tempo-focused boardAnythingThe game ends before it pays off

The pattern is simple: two Gambits that fire on different actions, on a board that only does one of those actions, means one of them is dead weight.
