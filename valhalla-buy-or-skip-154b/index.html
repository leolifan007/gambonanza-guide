Valhalla's Gambit Is $8 Now: Buy, Skip, or Wait for the Next Shop

Patch 1.5.4b cut Valhalla's Gambit from $10 to $8. A run-state decision table for buying it, skipping it, or saving for the board upgrade, without pretending to have run data we do not have.

Advertisement
MID GAME

The price moved from $10 to $8 and the temptation is to call that a buff. It is not. It is a discount, and the only question a discount changes is what else that money could have bought in the same shop.

**$2 is exactly one reroll.** Every shop between games offers one random piece, three random Gambits and three random tokens, with a $2 reroll. The Valhalla cut hands you back precisely one extra look.

THE ONE-SCREEN ANSWER

Buy when you already have the board state that makes the Gambit work and the boss preview does not demand a different answer. Skip when your five Gambit slots are the bottleneck. Wait when the preview or the Max Piece on Board upgrade is the better use of the same money.

The money and shop guide sets the baseline budget; this page is only the buy-or-skip decision for one Gambit at its new price.

Three Questions, In Order

A cheaper Gambit is still a Gambit, and you can hold five. The first filter is therefore not money, it is slots. If all five are occupied and none of them is expendable, $8 is no cheaper than $10 because there is nowhere to put it. The Gambits overview covers the cap and why selling is a real option in this game.

The second filter is board state. A Gambit that needs a specific board condition is only worth money if your board produces that condition. This is the same test the token decision guide applies to piece, Gambit and tile tokens: fix the problem you actually have, not the one a tier list was written for.

The third filter is the boss. Every shop before a boss game shows the next boss and its modifier in the lower-left corner, and the boss preview guide explains how to turn that into a purchase. A discount on a Gambit that does not answer the preview is still money spent on the wrong thing.

Quick reference, no table needed

On Free slot, supportive board, preview does not conflict: $8 leaves $2 for a reroll. On Five slots full, nothing worth selling: The bottleneck is slots, not cash. On Saving for Max Piece on Board: Compare against the upgrade payoff. On Preview shows a modifier that attacks a different resource: Spend on the counter the preview names. On Money at $8 - $9 total: It is now reachable, which is the only new fact.

What the $2 Actually Buys Back

  • One reroll. The shop reroll is $2, so a buy at $8 and a reroll costs what the Gambit alone used to cost.
  • One less forced sale. At $10 you sometimes sold a Gambit to afford it. At $8 that trade happens less often, and a sale now pays out correctly after the 1.5.4b Tooth fix.
  • Nothing else. The patch changed a price. It did not change the trigger, the effect, the rarity or the boss preview.

Do not turn a discount into a rule

There is no measured win rate in the official note and none is claimed here. If a guide tells you Valhalla’s Gambit is now ‘worth it in 68% of runs’, that number did not come from the 1.5.4b post. Decide from the five lines in the table above and your own board.

The same caution applies to the fix bundled in that post. Winning because an enemy fell used to leave a phantom piece on the board until the next victory; that was a bug and it is fixed. The cursed tile and tile control notes are a better place to think about board leftovers than a purchase decision.

The Version Check Nobody Does

If the shop still shows $10, your client is behind and every line of this decision is being applied to a different price. The check takes two seconds and belongs before the purchase, not after a bad run.

Pro tip:
Say the reason out loud
Before you click buy, name the board condition that will make the Gambit fire. If you cannot name it, you are buying the discount, not the Gambit.

For players still building a mental model of the shop loop, the how to play page covers the between-game shop, and the FAQ collects the common pricing and reroll questions in one place.
