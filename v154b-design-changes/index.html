1.5.4b Changed Two Rules, Not Twenty: The Rook-King Protection and the $2 Valhalla Cut

Patch 1.5.4b shipped six bug fixes but only two design changes: Mercenary's Gambit now protects both the ROOK and the KING, and Valhalla's Gambit dropped from $10 to $8. Here is what actually changed and what it does to your build.

Advertisement
MID GAME

If you skimmed the 1.5.4b notes and assumed the whole meta moved, it did not. Six lines in that post are bug fixes, two lines are design changes, and one is an art pass. Only two rules in the game actually behave differently today.

Mercenary’s Gambit now protects both pieces. The developer’s before-and-after line is exact: before, moving a ROOK ADJACENT to a KING protected it; after, moving a ROOK ADJACENT to a KING protects them both.

THE ONE-SCREEN ANSWER

Two changes, two consequences. Mercenary’s Gambit went from a one-piece shield to a two-piece shield, so an ADJACENT setup now pays off twice. Valhalla’s Gambit costs $8 instead of $10, so it competes with a reroll instead of competing with a slot. Everything else in 1.5.4b is a fix, not a rebalance.

The 1.5.0 to 1.5.3 hotfix breakdown covers the earlier reset rules; this page is only about the two design lines in the 30 September 2026 post.

The Rook-King Line, Read Literally

The patch note quotes the old wording and the new wording side by side. Before: moving a ROOK ADJACENT to a KING PROTECTS it. After: moving a ROOK ADJACENT to a KING PROTECTS them both. That is the entire mechanical change, and it is worth reading slowly because the rest of the paragraph is the developer explaining why.

The stated reason is that the Gambit was too situational. You needed three things to line up at once: a ROOK, an ADJACENT position, and a KING. Even with Anarchist’s Gambit in the mix, protecting a single piece was hard to make work. The fix keeps the identity and doubles the payoff.

If you already run ADJACENT effects, the ADJACENT keyword guide explains the positioning rule this Gambit depends on. The difference now is that a successful setup protects the piece doing the moving as well as the piece being protected, which changes the risk of committing a ROOK into a crowded board.

Do not read this as a new trigger

The trigger is unchanged. If your ROOK is not ADJACENT to a KING, nothing happens, exactly as before. Players who misread the note as ‘Mercenary’s Gambit now always protects two pieces’ will waste a Gambit slot waiting for an effect that never fires.

Why a $2 Price Cut Is a Real Decision Change

Valhalla’s Gambit dropped from $10 to $8. Two dollars sounds cosmetic until you put it next to the shop: there is a $2 reroll, one random piece, three random Gambits and three random tokens between games. Two dollars is exactly one reroll, so the patch moved this Gambit from ‘a full commitment’ to ‘a buy plus another look’.

The shop and economy guide covers how much you should be carrying into a boss game; the upgrade cost breakdown is the other side of the same budget. A $2 discount does not make Valhalla’s Gambit correct in every run, it just stops price from being the only reason you skip it.

  • Buy it when you already have the ROOK or KING density to make the protection chain pay, because $8 leaves room for a reroll.
  • Skip it when your five Gambit slots are the bottleneck, not your money. A cheaper Gambit still occupies a slot.
  • Wait when the boss preview shows a modifier that attacks a different resource. Spend on the answer, not on the discount.

What Players Keep Getting Wrong About This Patch

The art pass is not a balance pass. Eight Gambits got missing visual effects in 1.5.4b: Dainsleif’s, King XI’s, Resurrection Stone’s, Anna’s Family’s, Zen’s, Jockey’s, Princess’s and Warlock’s. Seeing an effect now does not mean the Gambit was buffed.

The same confusion hits the fix list. Barter’s Gambit showing a counter of 0 was a bug, not a nerf. Tooth’s Gambit not paying out on a sale was a bug, not a rules change. Gambit Medal progress vanishing after Quick Restart was a bug. None of these belong in a tier list, and the current tier list should not be rewritten on the strength of a fix.

Pro tip:
The 30-second version check
Open the shop and look at Valhalla’s Gambit. $8 means you are on 1.5.4b or later. $10 means your client is behind and none of the two design changes above apply to you yet.

If the shop offers you a Gambit you have not seen before, the three-token decision guide is the faster read than any tier list, because it starts from the state of your board instead of a ranking made for someone else’s run.
