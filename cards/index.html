Pieces, Not Cards

Gambonanza has no deck of cards and no hand to draw from. It uses chess pieces, and what you collect is Gambits that change how those pieces behave. This page covers the pieces themselves. For the Gambits, see the All Gambits Guide.

THE CORE IDEA

Every piece is good at something. A strong board is not a pile of the best pieces, it is pieces that cover each other’s weak spots and match the Gambits you hold.

How to Think About Each Piece

Pawns

Pawns are the cheapest way to turn money into board presence, and they are the fuel for a whole family of economy and promotion Gambits (Squirrel’s, Bug Catcher’s, Race Flag’s). On the cramped opening board, a pawn that reaches the far side promotes fast and becomes a real threat.

Pro tip:
Pawns are economy
A pawn-heavy opening plus pawn Gambits is one of the most reliable ways to keep gold flowing. See the Pawn Economy Loop for the full plan.

Knights

Knights shine on the small opening board, where their jump covers a large share of the squares. This is why the Knight Rush Opener works so well early. As the board grows, knights lose some of that edge and you lean on longer-range pieces instead.

Bishops

A bishop is dead weight until a diagonal opens. Once one does, it controls a whole line for free. On a cramped board, a bishop that cannot find a diagonal is a wasted slot.

Rooks

Rooks want space. They are slow to reposition on the opening board and become much stronger as the board grows and files clear out. Build for them mid-run, not on turn one.

The Queen

The queen is your carry. It is the strongest piece on the board, so the enemy AI and several bosses specifically target it. Protect it, and be ready to answer anything that threatens it. Beth’s and similar Gambits pay out around a queen that survives.

The King

The king is not your win condition. You win by capturing every enemy piece, so the king is just another piece on the board. Some Gambits involve the king (Throne’s and Wrecking Ball’s both grant you one), but you should not treat it as a protected objective the way chess does.

Building a Board That Covers Itself

If your board leans on…Add… to cover it
Slow pieces (rooks)Fast tempo (knights) to buy them time
A single strong piece (queen)Support pieces that can recapture after a trade
PawnsA promotion route and a pawn Gambit
Long-range piecesA piece that controls the squares they cannot see
AThe biggest board-building mistake is stacking one piece type because it is individually strong. A board where every piece supports the others wins more than a board of strong pieces pulling in different directions.
