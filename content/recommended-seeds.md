## The Opening Pool Comes From Your Collection

> There are no seed codes in Gambonanza. Your starting pieces come from the **Piece Wheel**, and the pool it draws from is your unlocked **Collection**. So the way to improve your openings is to widen your Collection and make better first decisions, not to type in a number.

{{< callout type="verdict" >}}<strong>THE SHORT VERSION</strong>

If you want a specific opening piece, the answer is not a seed, it is unlocking that piece and then using the Piece Wheel deliberately. This page explains how.{{< /callout >}}

The [Piece Wheel walkthrough](/best-seeds/) covers the spin itself. This page goes deeper on the Collection and on what your opening has to survive.

## What the Collection Unlocks

The Collection tracks what your future runs can offer you, across six categories. It totals around 200 entries, and every category changes a different part of the run.

<div class="synergy-table" style="overflow-x:auto">

| Category | What unlocking more does | When you feel it |
|----------|--------------------------|------------------|
| Gambits | Wider pool in your shop offerings | Every shop visit |
| Pieces | More shapes on the Piece Wheel at run start | The opening spin |
| Tiles | More tile modifiers available to buy | Mid run, when you shape the board |
| Enemy Modifiers | More variety in what you face | Later stages |
| Bosses | Progress toward the full boss roster | Boss checkpoints |
| Strains | Additional run variants | Once you unlock them |

</div>

Two of these matter far more than the rest early on. {{< game-icon name="grid" >}}**Pieces** decides what your opening spin can even offer you, and {{< game-icon name="casino" >}}**Gambits** decides what the shop can sell you. If your openings feel samey, Pieces is the category to grow. If your mid run feels weak, Gambits is. The [Collection unlock guide](/collection-unlock-guide/) covers how each category actually fills up.

{{< pro-tip >}}<strong>Unlock breadth beats unlock depth</strong><br>
  A Collection with many Pieces unlocked gives the Wheel more to choose from on every single run. A Collection with only a few unlocked gives you the same three shapes repeatedly, which is why early runs can feel identical.{{< /pro-tip >}}

## How a Run Is Structured

Your opening does not exist in isolation. It has to survive a fixed structure.

A full run is **5 groups of 5 games**, so roughly 25 games total. The **5th game of each group is a boss checkpoint**, which gives you 5 boss fights per run. Between games you visit the shop.

The board also grows as you go. You **start on a 5x5 board with 3 pieces**, and every time you clear a boss stage the board gains a row. That means the opening board is the most cramped board you will play, and your three starting pieces have to work in tight space.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}<strong>Why the opening board is the hard part</strong>

  On a 5x5 board with only three pieces, a Rook has no file to control and a Knight has limited targets. Pieces that are strong later can be dead weight at the start. Judge your opening by what it does now, not by what it becomes.{{< /callout >}}

If you want the full picture of what waits at each checkpoint, the [boss guide](/bosses/) lists all 8 bosses and their modifiers.

## How to Read a Good Opening

Since the reels draw from your Collection, a strong opening is really about fit, not luck.

<div class="synergy-table" style="overflow-x:auto">

| If you spin | Good first move | Watch out for |
|-------------|-----------------|---------------|
| A queen plus pawns | Buy a pawn-tempo Gambit and lean into promotion routes | Losing the queen early on a cramped board |
| Knights plus pawns | Play for fork threats while the board is small | Knights that cannot reach a target yet |
| Rooks plus pawns | Wait for a file to open, then control it | Slow starts before the board grows |
| All pawns | Take an economy or promotion Gambit early to scale fast | Thin board, low capture rate early |

</div>

The spin is a filter, not a sentence. If the three pieces you land cannot execute any plan, restarting costs almost nothing at that stage.

## Decisions That Beat the Spin

Your starting pieces set the tone. Your first three choices set the run.

1. **First Gambit:** pick one your board can actually trigger. Thunder's Gambit ($9) is the safest default with pawns active, because a pawn capture skips the enemy turn.
2. **First shop:** read the boss preview in the lower-left corner before buying. You are buying against a specific mechanic, not generically.
3. **Spending order:** survival and economy before raw power, because captured pieces are gone for the run.

The shop between games offers **one random piece, three random Gambits, and three random tokens**. Tokens come in three kinds: a piece token, a Gambit token that lets you pick a rarity and then choose one of three, and a tile token that modifies a tile you choose. Token tiers get more expensive and better.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}<strong>Read the boss preview first</strong>

  The next boss modifier is visible in the lower-left corner of the shop. That single piece of information should decide whether you spend on economy, on defence, or on a Gambit that counters the mechanic you are about to face.{{< /callout >}}

Because early money is tight and captured pieces never come back, the [early board economy guide](/early-board-economy/) is worth reading before you commit to a spending habit.

## Common Myths About Openings

- **"There is a best seed code."** No. The Piece Wheel draws from your Collection, and there is no code input.
- **"Ranked mode has different seeds."** Gambonanza is a single-player roguelike. There is no ranked ladder or PvP mode.
- **"Relics carry your run."** There is no relic system. Your run is Gambits, tiles, pieces, and money.
- **"The same start always plays the same."** The opening pool depends on your Collection and your choices, not a fixed number.

## Where Difficulty Fits In

Difficulty tiers unlock after your first clear, and there are six of them. Higher tiers add negative modifiers, and the highest tiers add a hidden sixth boss.

That matters for openings because a shape that is fine on the lowest tier can be unplayable once modifiers stack. All-pawn openings, for example, are cheap and fast to promote but fragile when a modifier punishes low-value pieces. The [difficulty guide](/difficulty-guide/) breaks down what each tier changes.
