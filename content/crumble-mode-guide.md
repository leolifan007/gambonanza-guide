Gambonanza is a game with two clocks. One is the board itself: **tiles fall away as a game runs**, taking any piece standing on them. The other is the **Stalemate Counter**, which ends a game outright if neither side moves the position forward. Together they make sure no game lasts forever.

{{< callout type="verdict" title="The Point of Both Timers" >}}
Crumble and the Stalemate Counter are not there to punish you randomly. They exist to stop stalling. A game where both players only shuffle pieces has to end somehow, and these two systems are that guarantee.
{{< /callout >}}

{{% section-divider %}}

## The Two Clocks

<div class="synergy-table" style="overflow-x:auto">

| Clock | What it does | How you stay ahead of it |
|-------|--------------|--------------------------|
| **Crumble** | Tiles drop from the board over time, taking pieces on them | Keep valuable pieces on solid ground and stay mobile |
| **Stalemate Counter** | Runs to 3/3, then the game ends | Keep the position moving, do not shuffle |

</div>

**Crumble** is a slow squeeze: the longer the game goes, the less board you have to work with.
**The Stalemate Counter** is a hard stop: three ticks and the game is over, whoever is "ahead" on material.

{{% section-divider %}}

## How to Read the Stalemate Counter

The Stalemate Counter goes to 3/3. When it fills, the game ends even if both sides still have pieces. That changes the shape of the endgame in three ways:

1. **You cannot win by hoarding.** If the counter fills, holding material advantage does not save you.
2. **Ahead on pieces means you want to trade.** If you have more material, simplifying the board protects your lead.
3. **Behind on pieces means you need action.** If you are down material, you need captures, not a standoff.

## Playing Under Crumble Pressure

### Early

- **Centralize valuable pieces** while there is still room.
- **Take economy Gambits early.** Gold now buys the pieces you will need when the board is tighter.
- **Do not spread to the outer tiles.** That is exactly where the collapse starts.

### Mid

- **Trade toward the middle.** A piece with a safe square behind it survives a collapse; a piece pinned to the edge does not.
- **Reassess slow pieces.** Rooks that never got a line are less useful as the board shrinks.
- **Keep the Stalemate Counter in mind.** If it is climbing and you are ahead, trade. If you are behind, attack.

### Late

- **Every move should do something.** Under both timers, a quiet move is a wasted turn.
- **Simplify a lead.** If you are ahead, meaningful trades protect the advantage.
- **Convert a deficit.** If you are behind, force captures before the counters end the game.

## Builds That Handle Pressure Well

1. **Tempo builds.** Ending the game before the counters matter is the cleanest answer.
2. **Pawn economy.** Cheap income that converts into better pieces, covered in the [Pawn Economy Loop](/pawn-economy-loop/).
3. **Capture-first boards.** A board that takes pieces every turn keeps both clocks irrelevant.

**Builds to be careful with:**
- Anything that needs lots of open space.
- Anything that pays off slowly, since both clocks punish slow games.

## Common Complaints, Honest Answers

Some players dislike how much the collapse influences a game. The fair version of that complaint is: when tiles fall, the board can shift in ways you did not choose. The honest answer is that Crumble is a clock you plan around, not a roll you react to:

1. **Keep pieces central.** Outer tiles fall first, so central pieces see the collapse coming.
2. **Stay mobile.** A piece with more than one good square is rarely trapped by a collapse.
3. **Do not over-commit to one piece early.** Keep options open until the board stabilizes.
4. **Treat both timers as the reason to act.** Pressure is the mode, not a bug.
