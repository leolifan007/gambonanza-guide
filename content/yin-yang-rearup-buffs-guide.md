The short version: Gambonanza 1.4.0 quietly turned three Gambits into real picks. Yin's Gambit and Yang's Gambit both moved from a 1/3 trigger to a 1/2 trigger, so they now fire almost 50 percent more often. Rear Up's Gambit went from +$2 to +$4 on promotion, which doubles the economy you get every time you promote a piece.

{{< callout type="verdict" title="Quick Takeaway" >}}
Run Yin and Yang together for steady triggers. Run Rear Up in any promotion build. Both are cheap, both scale with run length.
{{< /callout >}}

{{< diagram src="buffs-before-after.svg" alt="Yin, Yang, and Rear Up buffs before versus after 1.4" caption="Yin and Yang move from 1/3 to 1/2; Rear Up pays +$4 instead of +$2." >}}

## The Three Buffs at a Glance

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
**Yin and Yang**: 1/3 -> **1/2** trigger chance. **Rear Up**: +$2 -> **+$4** per promotion. That is the whole headline.
{{< /callout >}}

| Gambit | Before | After | Impact |
|--------|--------|-------|--------|
| Yin's Gambit | 1/3 chance | **1/2 chance** | Fires almost 50% more often |
| Yang's Gambit | 1/3 chance | **1/2 chance** | Same reliability bump |
| Rear Up's Gambit | +$2 per promotion | **+$4 per promotion** | Doubles promotion economy |

The key point: **Yin and Yang are triggers**. **Rear Up is economy**. They solve different problems, which is why they pair cleanly.

## Why Yin and Yang Matter More Now

At 1/3, a conditional Gambit felt like a tax. You would set up the board, meet the condition, and still watch two out of three triggers fizzle.

At 1/2, the math flips:
- **Before**: Right less than half the time
- **After**: Wrong less than half the time

For a Gambit you build your whole run around, that is the line between a gimmick and a plan.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
Run both Yin and Yang together. They are independent rolls, so your "at least one fires" rate is **3 out of 4 triggers**. That is the consistency capture-focused builds were missing.
{{< /callout >}}

## Rear Up Doubles Your Promotion Economy

Rear Up was always a nice idea: get paid for promoting. The problem was the payout.

{{< callout type="verdict" >}}
**Before**: +$2 per promotion -> rounding error
**After**: +$4 per promotion -> recurring income stream

Promotion stops being a one-time bump and starts being a tempo tool that pays you to use it.
{{< /callout >}}

This matters because v1.4.0 also made **promote-into-PAWN** possible. You can now chain promotions to keep the Rear Up cash flowing while you reshape your board.

## Two Archetypes to Abuse

**Archetype 1: The Yin + Yang Trigger Engine**

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
- Slot both Yin's Gambit and Yang's Gambit early
- Build your board to meet both trigger conditions consistently
- Expect a payoff on **3 of every 4 triggers** instead of 1 of 3
- Pair with Gambits that create more trigger opportunities
{{< /callout >}}

**Archetype 2: The Rear Up Promotion Economy**

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
- Slot Rear Up's Gambit in any build that promotes pieces
- Use promote-into-PAWN to chain promotions without committing to a single strong piece
- Reinvest the +$4 per promotion into shop visits and tile upgrades
- Scales hardest in long PAWN-difficulty runs
{{< /callout >}}

You can run both archetypes in the same build if your board supports promotions and trigger conditions at once. They do not fight for the same resources.

## A Grounded Opener Skeleton

| Phase | Priority | Why |
|-------|----------|-----|
| Turns 1-2 | Secure Yin and Yang | Do not force Rear Up yet |
| Turns 3-4 | Shape board for trigger conditions | Stop wasting rolls |
| Mid run | Add Rear Up if you have a promotion plan | Especially with promote-into-PAWN |
| Late run | Chain promotions for +$4 each | Recover losses via Graveyard |

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
On PAWN difficulty, lean into Rear Up harder. The Graveyard lets you buy back lost pieces, so you can promote aggressively and keep the economy rolling.
{{< /callout >}}

## Should You Pick These Up

{{< callout type="verdict" title="Who Should Grab These" >}}
- **Pick up Yin and Yang** if you run a capture or condition-focused board and want reliability
- **Pick up Rear Up** if your build promotes pieces at all (+$4 is free value)
- **Casual PAWN players** get the most out of all three because the Graveyard removes the risk
{{< /callout >}}

The honest ceiling: none of these three are broken. They are buffed, not reworked. But in a patch where most changes were features and QoL, free reliability and free economy are the easiest wins you can take.

## Rear Up +$4 in Practice: Turn-by-Turn Economy

{{< callout type="tip" title="Why +$4 vs +$2 Matters" >}}
If Rear Up fires **3 times** in a run:
- Old value: +$6 breathing room
- New value: +$12 breathing room

On a board where a key piece costs $5 to recover, that extra $6 is the difference between a stalled economy and a recovered board.
{{< /callout >}}

Pair Rear Up with Yin and Yang (now 1/2 instead of 1/3) and the economy swings compound: more trigger events, more cash, more recovery. That is why these three buffs are best read as one package.
