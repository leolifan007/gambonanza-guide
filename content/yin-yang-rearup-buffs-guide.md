The short version: Gambonanza 1.4.0 quietly turned three Gambits into real picks. Yin's Gambit and Yang's Gambit both moved from a 1/3 trigger to a 1/2 trigger, so they now fire almost 50 percent more often. Rear Up's Gambit went from +$2 to +$4 on promotion, which doubles the economy you get every time you promote a piece. None of these are flashy, but all three are strictly better than they were, and together they enable two archetypes that were awkward before the patch: a consistent trigger-pair economy and a promotion-tempo money engine.

If you want the one-line takeaway, here it is. Run Yin and Yang together for steady, repeatable triggers, and run Rear Up whenever your build leans on promoting pieces for tempo or value. Both are cheap to slot, neither competes for the same board space, and both scale with how long your run lasts.

{{< callout type="verdict" title="The Three Buffs, Before and After" >}}
Yin's Gambit: 1/3 trigger to 1/2 trigger. Yang's Gambit: 1/3 trigger to 1/2 trigger. Rear Up's Gambit: +$2 to +$4 on promotion. That is the whole headline. The rest is about how to actually use the extra uptime and the extra cash.
{{< /callout >}}

## The Three Buffs at a Glance

Here are the exact numbers from the v1.4.0 patch, no guessing involved.

| Gambit | What It Does (high level) | Before | After | Why the Buff Matters |
|--------|---------------------------|--------|-------|----------------------|
| Yin's Gambit | Triggers on its condition at a dice-style rate for value or board effect | 1/3 chance | **1/2 chance** | Fires far more reliably, turning it from a coin flip into a near-certain payoff. |
| Yang's Gambit | Same family of conditional trigger, mirrors Yin for a second angle | 1/3 chance | **1/2 chance** | Same reliability bump. The two stack into a consistent trigger engine. |
| Rear Up's Gambit | Pays you money each time you promote a piece | +$2 | **+$4** | Promotion economy is now doubled, making promote-tempo builds viable. |

The key point is that Yin and Yang are triggers, while Rear Up is economy. They solve different problems, which is exactly why they pair so cleanly.

## Why Yin and Yang Matter More Now

At 1/3, a conditional Gambit felt like a tax you paid hoping for a payoff. You would set up the board, meet the condition, and still watch two out of three triggers fizzle. At 1/2, the math flips. You are now wrong less than half the time instead of right less than half the time. For a Gambit you are building your whole run around, that difference is the line between a gimmick and a plan.

The reason to run both at once is simple: they are independent rolls. If each fires on its own 1/2 check, your combined "at least one fires" rate is 3 out of 4 triggers. That is the consistency that capture-focused and condition-focused builds were missing. You stop praying for your engine to come online and start expecting it.

Do not overthink which of the two to pick if you only have room for one. They are siblings in this patch. The buff is identical, so the choice comes down to which condition fits your board. If you can support both, support both.

## Rear Up Doubles Your Promotion Economy

Rear Up was always a nice idea: get paid for promoting. The problem was the payout. Two dollars per promotion is a rounding error in a run where a single shop visit costs more than that. At +$4, promotion stops being a one-time bump and starts being a recurring income stream.

This matters because v1.4.0 also made promote-into-PAWN possible. That change means you are no longer forced to promote only into high-value pieces. You can chain promotions, including into PAWN, to keep the Rear Up cash flowing while you reshape your board. Promotion is no longer just an endgame power spike. It is a tempo tool that pays you to use it.

The build this enables is a promote-tempo economy: keep promoting, keep cashing Rear Up, and reinvest the income into more pieces or better tiles. The longer the run goes, the more promotions you get, and the more Rear Up pulls ahead of flat-income Gambits.

## Builds to Abuse

Two clean archetypes fall out of these buffs.

**The Yin + Yang Trigger Engine**
- Slot both Yin's Gambit and Yang's Gambit early.
- Build your board to meet both trigger conditions as often as possible.
- Expect a payoff on roughly 3 of every 4 triggers instead of 1 of every 3.
- Pair with Gambits or tiles that create more trigger opportunities, not fewer.

**The Rear Up Promotion Economy**
- Slot Rear Up's Gambit in any build that promotes pieces.
- Use promote-into-PAWN to chain promotions without committing your board to a single strong piece.
- Reinvest the +$4 per promotion into shop visits and tile upgrades.
- This scales hardest in long PAWN-difficulty runs where the Graveyard keeps your pieces alive.

You can run both archetypes in the same build if your board supports promotions and trigger conditions at once. They do not fight for the same resources.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}
If you are on PAWN difficulty, lean into Rear Up harder. The Graveyard (new in 1.4.0) lets you buy back lost pieces, so you can promote aggressively, recover what you snipe, and keep the promotion economy rolling without the usual downside risk.
{{< /callout >}}

## A Grounded Opener Skeleton

This is a build skeleton, not a scripted line. The numbers below are only the verified buffs; do not treat the turn order as exact.

1. **Turns 1-2:** Secure Yin's Gambit and Yang's Gambit if they appear. Do not force Rear Up yet unless you already plan to promote.
2. **Turns 3-4:** Shape your board to meet the Yin and Yang conditions consistently. The goal is to stop wasting trigger rolls.
3. **Mid run:** Add Rear Up's Gambit once you have a promotion plan, especially if promote-into-PAWN is available.
4. **Late run:** Chain promotions for +$4 each, recover any losses through the Graveyard, and reinvest into tiles that create more trigger chances.

The skeleton works because every step uses only verified mechanics. Yin and Yang give you reliability, Rear Up gives you the cash to keep the loop alive, and the Graveyard covers your mistakes.

## Should You Pick These Up

{{< callout type="verdict" title="Who Should Grab These" >}}
Pick up Yin and Yang if you already run a capture or condition-focused board and want reliability without reworking your setup. Pick up Rear Up if your build promotes pieces at all, since +$4 is free value on top of what you were already doing. Casual PAWN players get the most out of all three because the Graveyard removes the risk of promoting aggressively.
{{< /callout >}}

The honest ceiling: none of these three are broken. They are buffed, not reworked. But in a patch where most changes were features and QoL, free reliability and free economy are the easiest wins you can take. Slot them, build around the triggers, and let the doubled promotion payout carry the late game.
