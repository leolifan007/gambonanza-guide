Three tokens sit in every shop and most players grab the cheapest one. That is usually backwards.

{{< game-icon name="casino" >}}**A Gambit token lets you pick a rarity and then choose one of three.** That is the only token in the shop where you control what you get twice. A piece token gives you a random piece. A tile token gives you a random tile modifier, but you choose which square it lands on.

{{< callout type="verdict" >}}<strong>THE FIVE-SECOND TEST</strong>

If your board cannot trigger the Gambits you already own, take the Gambit token. If your board is thin and losing pieces, take the piece token. If the board shape is the problem and the pieces are fine, take the tile token.{{< /callout >}}

## What Each Token Actually Gives You

The three tokens are not three flavours of the same thing. They solve three different problems, which is why "which is best" has no fixed answer.

{{< game-icon name="grid" >}}**Piece token** - one random piece from the pool. It is the most replaceable of the three, because pieces also come from the shop's direct piece slot and from the [Piece Wheel at run start](/recommended-seeds/).

{{< game-icon name="bolt" >}}**Gambit token** - you choose a rarity tier, then pick one of three Gambits in that tier. This is the highest-control option in the shop and the one that most often fixes a broken build.

{{< game-icon name="shield" >}}**Tile token** - a random tile modifier, but you pick the square. The modifier is random, the placement is not, which makes it the best token for fixing a specific problem area of the board.

| Token | What is random | What you control | Best when |
|-------|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| Piece | The piece | Nothing | Your board is thin or has lost key pieces |
| Gambit | The three offered | Rarity, then the pick | Your Gambits no longer fire |
| Tile | The modifier | Which square it lands on | The board shape is costing you games |

## Decide by Run State, Not by Tier

Tokens come in tiers and get more expensive as they get better, but the tier matters less than what your run currently needs. Judge the board, then spend.

### Take the Gambit token when your Gambits have gone quiet

This is the most common real problem. You bought Gambits early, the board changed, and now nothing triggers.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}<strong>The tell</strong>

If you finish a game and realise none of your Gambits fired, that is not bad luck, it is a mismatch. A Gambit token lets you pick a rarity and then choose one of three, so it is the most reliable way to add a trigger your board can actually use.{{< /callout >}}

The [Gambits guide](/gambits/) lists trigger conditions if you are unsure whether what you own still fits.

### Take the piece token when the board is thin

Pieces are the win condition in both directions: you lose when all your pieces are gone, and you win by capturing everything. A thin board is an emergency, and no Gambit fixes it.

{{< pro-tip >}}<strong>Count before you spend</strong><br>
  If you are down to three or four pieces and the next fight is a boss, the piece token is not a value decision, it is survival. Gambits do nothing if the piece that triggers them is already gone.{{< /pro-tip >}}

### Take the tile token when the board shape is the problem

This is the one players underuse. If your pieces are fine and your Gambits fire, but you keep losing specific squares or specific trades, the board itself is the issue.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}<strong>Why tile tokens punch above their price</strong>

The modifier is random but the square is yours. That means you can put a Protective Tile on the square your queen keeps dying on, or a Trap Tile on the route the enemy keeps using. Precision beats power when you know exactly where the problem is.{{< /callout >}}

## The Mistake That Costs the Most

{{< game-icon name="warning" >}}**Buying the token before reading the boss preview.** The shop shows the next boss in the lower-left corner, and it changes which token is correct.

Against Tal the Cursed, which places five cursed squares that demote whatever steps on them, a tile token does very little, because your tile is competing with five bad ones. Against Kev Borclick, which puts two of your pieces into STASIS every turn, a piece token is worth more, because two of your bodies are frozen and you need spares.

{{< game-icon name="check" >}}**Order of operations:** read the preview first, name the resource under attack, then pick the token that restores it. The [boss preview guide](/reading-boss-preview-shop/) walks through that read step by step.

## What About the Money

Tokens cost money and the tiers get expensive, so the token decision is also a budget decision. Early money is tight, and pieces you lose are gone for the run.

{{< callout type="info" >}}<strong>The honest trade-off</strong>

The best tier you can afford is not always the right buy. A cheap piece token that keeps you alive is worth more than an expensive Gambit token you cannot afford alongside the piece you also need. The [money and shop guide](/economy/) covers how much to carry into a boss game.{{< /callout >}}

If you are deciding between a token and saving, remember that the shop also sells one piece and three Gambits directly: compare all seven slots before you commit.
