Most players shop for power. The shop in Gambonanza is not asking you to buy power, it is asking you to buy an answer, and it tells you the question first.

{{< game-icon name="warning" >}}**The preview sits in the lower-left corner of the shop**, before you spend anything. It shows which boss is next and what that boss does to the board. Read it, then spend. If you skip it, you are buying blind against a rule you could have seen.

{{< callout type="verdict" >}}<strong>THE ONE-SCREEN ANSWER</strong>

Read the boss preview, name the rule it imposes, and buy the thing that answers that rule. A cheap counter to the boss in front of you beats an expensive upgrade that does nothing about it.{{< /callout >}}

The [full boss roster](/bosses/) lists all 8 modifiers in detail. This page is about what to do with the information in the two seconds before you click buy.

## Why the Preview Exists

A run is 5 groups of 5 games, and the 5th game of each group is a boss checkpoint. That is 5 boss fights, and you get a shop visit before every one of them.

{{< game-icon name="grid" >}}**Boss order is not fixed.** Which of the 8 bosses you face, and in what order, changes between runs. That is exactly why the preview exists: without it, you would have no way to prepare for a mechanic you cannot predict.

{{< section-divider >}}

## The Shop You Are Spending In

Every shop between games offers the same shape of inventory, so the decision is always the same kind of decision.

{{< game-icon name="money" >}}**One random piece**, **three random Gambits**, and **three random tokens**. Tokens come in three kinds: a piece token, a Gambit token where you pick a rarity and then choose one of three, and a tile token that modifies a tile you select. Token tiers get pricier and better.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}<strong>The budget is the constraint</strong>

You rarely have enough for everything. That is the point. The preview tells you which of those seven slots actually matters this time, and the [money and shop guide](/economy/) covers how much you should be carrying into a boss game in the first place.{{< /callout >}}

## What Each Boss Asks You to Buy

Since the preview names the boss, you can map each modifier to a purchase. This is the part most players never do.

| If the preview shows | The rule it imposes | Buy this instead of raw power |
|---------------------|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| Hikarul the Banished | You cannot use Stock | Board-wide survivability, since you cannot replace losses |
| Botezarro | Capturing on the masked color cracks that square | Pieces that do not depend on one color |
| Jawby Fisher | Every capture you take destroys a random Stock piece | Defence, so you take fewer captures |
| Judit Polgeisha | You cannot see the enemy formation in staging | Flexible pieces over setup-dependent ones |
| Kev Borclick | Two of your pieces get STASIS each turn | Extra bodies, because two are frozen |
| M3CH4GNU5 C4RL53N | Your own pieces get shuffled and hidden in staging | Gambits that trigger on move, not on position |
| Tal the Cursed | Five cursed squares that demote what steps on them | A tile token, so you can answer the board |
| Mighty Kasparov | The hardest modifier, but the most predictable pieces | Whatever you have been saving for |

{{< game-icon name="check" >}}**The pattern:** every boss attacks a resource. Hikarul attacks your Stock, Jawby attacks it too, Kev Borclick attacks your piece count, and M3CH4GNU5 attacks your setup. Buy back the resource being attacked.

## Two Bosses That Punish the Same Habit

The clearest example is the Stock pair. Hikarul removes Stock entirely, and Jawby Fisher destroys a random Stock piece every time something of yours is captured.

{{< callout type="danger" >}}<strong>The habit that loses both</strong>

Entering with a thin board and a fat Stock, planning to feed pieces in as you lose them. Against Hikarul you cannot feed. Against Jawby, feeding costs you a random reserve piece each time. The same plan fails twice.{{< /callout >}}

The fix is the same for both: arrive with the board already working. Pieces on the board, not in reserve.

## Where Players Get This Wrong

{{< game-icon name="help" >}}**Three mistakes account for most boss losses**, and none of them are tactical.

1. **Buying before looking.** The preview is on screen the whole time. Players spend first and read second.
2. **Buying generically.** A strong Gambit that needs setup is weak against M3CH4GNU5, which shuffles your setup.
3. **Saving for later.** Money does not help you if the run ends. Spend against the boss you can see, not the one you hope comes next.

{{< pro-tip >}}<strong>The two-second check</strong><br>
  Before you click anything in a pre-boss shop, say out loud which resource the boss attacks. If you cannot name it, you have not read the preview yet. That single habit is worth more than any Gambit ranking.{{< /pro-tip >}}

## Does Difficulty Change the Preview

Difficulty tiers unlock after your first clear, there are six, and higher tiers stack negative modifiers. The preview still shows the boss, so the read stays the same, but the margin for a wrong buy shrinks.

The [boss prep guide](/boss-strategy-guide/) goes deeper on what to carry into each of the five checkpoints, and the [Gambits guide](/gambits/) helps when the shop offers you a Gambit you have not seen before.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}<strong>On the highest tiers</strong>

The top tiers add a hidden sixth boss. The preview mechanic still applies, so the habit does not change, only the stakes.{{< /callout >}}
