The upgrade looks boring and it quietly decides runs. Max Piece on Board sets how many of your pieces can be on the board at once, and in a game where you lose when every piece is gone, that number is your margin.

{{< game-icon name="grid" >}}**The catch is the price.** Each purchase raises the cost of the next one, so the first upgrade is cheap and the fourth is not. That curve is the whole decision: you are not asking whether more pieces are good, you are asking whether *this* increment is worth *this* price.

{{< callout type="verdict" >}}<strong>THE SHORT ANSWER</strong>

Buy the first upgrade early, almost always. Buy the second if your board is usually full. Skip the third and beyond unless you have money to burn and a build that actually uses the space.{{< /callout >}}

## Why the Cap Matters More Than It Looks

You start a run on a 5x5 board with 3 pieces, which is deliberately cramped. The board gains a row every time you clear a boss stage, so space opens up as the run goes on.

Two rules make the cap central.

{{< game-icon name="shield" >}}**You lose when all your pieces are gone.** Not when your king falls, because there is no king to fall. Every extra piece on the board is one more capture the enemy has to make.

{{< game-icon name="trending" >}}**You win by capturing everything.** More bodies means more attackers, more blockers, and more Gambit triggers, since most Gambits need a piece to do something.

{{< section-divider >}}

## The Board Has to Be Big Enough

This is the part players miss. The cap only helps if the board can hold the pieces you are paying to field.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}<strong>Space first, then bodies</strong>

On a 5x5 board with three pieces, a fourth piece is fighting for room with the three you already have. Once the board has grown past a boss or two, that same fourth piece has somewhere useful to stand. The upgrade gets better as the run goes on, which is the opposite of how most upgrades work.{{< /callout >}}

So the honest read is that **the early upgrade is an investment in the late board**, not a fix for the early one. You buy it cheap now because it is expensive later.

## What You Are Actually Trading Against

Every coin on the cap is a coin not spent on something else, and the shop is not generous.

| Spend it on | Buys you | Best when |
|-------------|----------|-----------|
| Max Piece on Board | More bodies, more attackers | Your board is full and you still have money |
| A Gambit | A new trigger or effect | Your current Gambits have stopped firing |
| A piece | An immediate body | You are thin right now and might not survive |
| A tile token | A fix for one specific square | One area of the board keeps costing you |

{{< game-icon name="money" >}}**The rule that settles most of these:** if you are about to lose pieces this fight, buy pieces. If you are not, buy capacity. The cap is a luxury you buy when you are stable, not a rescue.

The [money and shop guide](/economy/) covers where the gold comes from and what the shop offers alongside this upgrade.

## The Landing Cost Nobody Counts

Pieces do not appear on the board for free. Stock pieces sit off-board and deploying one costs a turn.

{{< callout type="warning" >}}<strong>The hidden cost of a bigger cap</strong>

Raising the cap does not put pieces on the board, it only lets you. Each one still costs a turn to deploy, and turns are exactly what a boss fight does not give you for free. A higher cap with no time to fill it is money spent on nothing.{{< /callout >}}

That is the strongest argument against chasing the expensive later tiers. The board grows, but your turn count does not.

## A Practical Buying Rule

{{< game-icon name="check" >}}**Three checks before you click the upgrade**, in this order.

1. **Is my board actually full?** If you routinely have pieces sitting in Stock with nowhere to go, the cap is the bottleneck and you should buy. If your board is rarely full, the cap is not your problem.
2. **Am I about to face a boss?** Before a boss fight, money is usually better spent on survival. Buy the cap after the checkpoint, not before it. The [boss preview guide](/reading-boss-preview-shop/) covers reading what is coming.
3. **Do I have a build that uses bodies?** Promotion builds and pawn builds convert extra pieces into real value. A build centred on one or two strong pieces gets almost nothing from a wider board.

{{< pro-tip >}}<strong>The early-buy exception</strong><br>
  The first upgrade is cheap enough that waiting is usually a mistake, even if your board is not full yet. You are buying the cheap tier of a curve that only gets steeper, so the discount is in the timing rather than the effect.{{< /pro-tip >}}

## Where Stock Fits

Stock holds up to 7 extra pieces off-board, and there is a separate upgrade for how many you can deploy per turn. That matters here because the cap and Stock do different jobs.

{{< callout type="info" >}}<strong>Cap versus Stock</strong>

The cap is how many can be *on* the board. Stock is how many you have *waiting*. If your Stock is full and your board is full, the cap is the limit. If your board is full and your Stock is empty, more cap buys you nothing until you get pieces. Check which one is actually empty before spending.{{< /callout >}}
