Winning the opening is not the hard part of Gambonanza. The hard part is the last few pieces, when the board is small, the Crumble counter is running, the Stalemate Counter is creeping toward its limit, and one wrong capture hands the game back. I have lost more runs in the final three pieces than in every boss fight combined. This guide is everything I do at the end of a match to actually finish it.

{{< callout type="verdict" >}}<strong>THE ENDGAME RULE</strong>

The endgame is not a damage race. It is an ordering problem. You are deciding the sequence in which the last enemy pieces come off the board, while the Crumble counter and the Stalemate Counter both push you to move faster than your plan allows. Get the order right and the win is mechanical. Get it wrong and a won game ends in a loss with both timers still on zero.{{< /callout >}}

## What Actually Happens at the End

A few facts drive every decision below.

- **You win by capturing all enemy pieces.** There is no check or mate, and the enemy King is captured like any other piece. The moment the last enemy piece leaves the board, you have won.
- **A last piece triggers Crumble.** When either side is reduced to a single piece, the Crumble counter starts, and from then on the board loses ground every turn, taking tiles and any pieces standing on them.
- **The Stalemate Counter ends games.** If neither side makes real progress for long enough, it reaches 3/3 and the game is over. Turtling is not a valid endgame plan.
- **Some pieces cannot be captured early.** Elite pieces can only be taken last, and bosses carry their own protected piece with the same rule.
- **Some pieces damage the board when captured.** Crumbler pieces take tiles with them when they go down, and that changes whether a capture is good or bad.

Every tip here is a consequence of the facts above.

## Tip 1: Recognize the One-Piece Crumble Before It Starts

The moment a side drops to one piece, Crumble begins, and the board starts coming apart. This is the most important transition in the game and most players notice it one turn too late.

### When you are the one down to one piece

This is survival mode. Your jobs, in order:

1. **Do not let the last piece sit still.** Crumble is eating your position regardless of what you do, so static play just lets the board shrink under you.
2. **Get a piece onto the board.** If you have anything in Stock, this is the moment Landing is worth the full turn it costs. A second body stops you being one capture away from a lost run, and it can often stall the countdown logic by giving you something to work with.
3. **Take a capture if one exists.** Removing an enemy piece both thins their net and forces progress, which keeps the Stalemate Counter from climbing while you are already under pressure.
4. **Stay central if the board allows it.** Center tiles survive board shrink longer than edges, so on a collapsing board the middle is worth more than a "better" edge square.

Recovering from one piece is possible but it is a narrow window. The first turn after Crumble starts is the one that matters most.

### When the opponent is down to one piece

This is the conversion. Everything below is about not letting that one piece slip out.

{{< section-divider >}}

## Tip 2: Trap the Last Enemy Piece

When the opponent has one piece left, that piece triggers Crumble for them too, so the board is actively shrinking around both of you. Your job is to net it before either timer or an escape gives it back.

How I net a single piece:

- **Cut its squares, not its escape squares.** Do not chase it. Move pieces so that the squares it wants to step onto are all attacked. Chasing pushes it away. Cutting removes the door.
- **Close the board, then close the ring.** The shrinking board is your ally here. Let Crumble remove the outer tiles while you hold the middle, and the last piece runs out of squares rather than out of luck.
- **Leave one square, then punish it.** If you can attack every escape square except one, the piece will take the last square, and that is exactly where you want it. This works with any piece type and does not require a rare Gambit.
- **Do not capture it with the wrong piece.** If the opponent has any remaining way to punish your capturing piece next turn, the win can reverse. Take the piece with something that cannot be punished.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}<strong>THE ONE-SQUARE RULE</strong>

A lone enemy piece has very few legal squares. Count them out loud. If it has two squares and you attack one, it must take the other. That tells you your next move before the opponent makes it. Endgames against a single piece are solved positions, not guessing games.{{< /callout >}}

## Tip 3: Manage the Stalemate Counter at 2/3

The Stalemate Counter is the timer that punishes nothing happening. At 3/3 the game simply ends, and if you were ahead, that is a loss of everything you built. The counter is measured in progress, not in turns alone - captures and real positional change count, shuffling does not.

My rules for the counter:

- **At 2/3, I buy progress immediately.** I do not wait for a perfect capture. A capture, or a forced threat that makes the opponent respond, is what resets the clock.
- **A capture threat counts.** If my move forces the opponent to deal with a piece next turn, that is progress even before the capture lands.
- **Trading when ahead is fine.** If I have three pieces and they have two, an even trade makes it two versus one, and that is progress toward the win. Trades that reduce their count are never wasted.
- **Aimless repositioning is the enemy.** Moving a piece back and forth to "improve" it is exactly the behavior that pushes the counter to 3/3. Every endgame turn should either take a piece or create the threat of one.

{{< callout type="danger" >}}<strong>THE COMFORTABLE STALEMATE</strong>

The most dangerous endgame state is when you are winning comfortably and both sides are just shuffling. It feels safe because nothing bad is happening. Then the counter hits 3/3 and the run is gone. If the counter is at 2/3 and you are ahead, force the issue now. A slightly risky capture to force progress beats a safe shuffle that ends the game in a draw-like loss.{{< /callout >}}

## Tip 4: Handle Elite Pieces and Boss Pieces Correctly

Elite pieces cannot be captured until they are the last piece standing, and boss encounters give the boss its own protected piece with the same restriction. This is not a suggestion, it is a hard rule of the game, and fighting it wastes turns.

How I plan around it:

- **Count protectors before you count pieces.** When you look at the remaining enemy pieces, mentally set aside every Elite and any boss-protected piece. The real target list is only the normal pieces.
- **Clear the normal pieces first, always.** Every turn spent attacking an Elite is a turn the Crumble counter uses against you. Chipping at something that cannot fall is the definition of wasted tempo.
- **Use the protected piece's immobility.** An Elite or boss piece that cannot be captured is also, in a way, easy to ignore. You can park a piece where it is not a threat and focus entirely on the rest of the board.
- **Plan the final capture in advance.** When only the Elite is left, you are allowed to take it, and that is the win. But make sure you are the one setting up that position, not the one being dragged into it.

{{< section-divider >}}

## Tip 5: Treat Crumbler Pieces as Timing Decisions

Crumbler pieces damage the board when they are captured, taking tiles with them. That means a capture is not only about removing a piece, it is also about when and where the board breaks.

- **Capture Crumbler pieces on your schedule.** If a Crumbler is going to damage the board eventually, you would rather be the one who decides when. Taking it on your terms beats being forced to take it mid-plan.
- **Prefer the center when it goes down.** If you cannot avoid a Crumbler collapsing tiles, try to have the collapse happen away from where your remaining pieces need to stand.
- **Watch what is standing near the Crumbler.** If one of your pieces is on a tile the Crumbler would take, move it first, then capture.
- **In crammed endgames, a Crumbler capture can win you the game by shrinking the board to a size your pieces dominate.** On a small collapsing board, capture order matters more than material count.

## Tip 6: Finish with Piece Types That Close Well

Not every piece is good at converting a small board. In my runs, these are the pieces that actually close out matches:

### Queen

The Queen is the best closer in the game because it attacks along every line, so its net covers the most escape squares at once. In a small endgame, a Queen can cover almost an entire ring by itself. If you have a Queen alive at the end, build the trap around her and use your other pieces to cover the squares she cannot.

### Rook

The Rook is the workhorse of the last few turns. It controls a full row and a full column, which makes it excellent at cutting off escape lanes on a small board. Park it on the line the last piece wants to travel along and it does the job of several pieces.

### Promoted pieces

Promotion is a real endgame weapon. A Pawn that reaches the far rank becomes a much stronger piece, and that swing can turn a stalemate into a win instantly. If you have a Pawn close to promoting when the Crumble starts, seriously consider pushing it, because a fresh Queen on a tiny board usually ends the game on the spot. Note that some Gambits pay you for promoting, and some let a Pawn promote the moment it Lands, so check your Gambit list before you assume the promotion is too slow.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}<strong>PROMOTION IS A TEMPO PLAY</strong>

Teams underestimate promotion in the endgame because it looks slow. But promoting is often the fastest way to create progress against a Stalemate Counter, since the resulting piece changes the position completely. If the counter is climbing and a promotion is two turns away, the promotion may be the more reliable win than a risky capture.{{< /callout >}}

## Tip 8: Common Ways I Have Thrown a Won Endgame

<div class="synergy-table" style="overflow-x:auto">

| Loss | What happened | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Chased the last piece** | I pushed it around the board and it kept escaping | Cut its squares instead; let the shrinking board do the work |
| **Waited one turn too many** | I had a winning line and let the Stalemate Counter hit 3/3 | At 2/3, commit. Progress is mandatory |
| **Attacked the Elite** | I spent turns on a piece that cannot be captured yet | Clear normal pieces first, Elites last |
| **Let the Crumbler break my square** | I captured a Crumbler and lost a piece to the collapse | Move first, then capture |
| **Held Stock instead of Landing** | I was down to one piece with pieces in reserve and never deployed | The first turn after Crumble starts is the Landing turn |
| **Got greedy on the final capture** | I took the last piece with something that got recaptured | Take the winning piece with a piece that cannot be punished |

</div>

{{< callout type="danger" >}}<strong>THE GREEDY FINAL CAPTURE</strong>

The single most painful endgame loss is taking the last enemy piece with a piece that then gets captured, or worse, taking a near-final piece when a full capture was available and ending up in a scramble. Before you take what looks like the winning capture, confirm it actually ends the game. If it does not, confirm it does not hand back a capture next turn. Two seconds of checking beats a lost run.{{< /callout >}}

## Tip 9: Endgames Against Bosses

Boss fights have their own endgame quirks because each boss changes one rule. What matters at the end of a boss match is not the boss's damage, it is which of your endgame tools the boss has disabled.

- **Hikarul the Banished blocks Stock**, so you cannot rely on Landing a rescue piece once you are down to one. In this fight, keep more pieces alive into the endgame than you would normally.
- **Jawby Fisher destroys a random Stock piece when captured**, so Landing a big reserve against it is unreliable. Keep your board pieces protected instead of trusting Stock.
- **Kev Borclick applies Stasis** so your pieces cannot capture for a stretch. That means your endgame plan must survive a turn or two where you cannot take pieces, which is exactly when the Stalemate Counter is dangerous.
- **Tàl the Cursed floods the board with Cursed Tiles** that demote pieces to Pawns. In the endgame, avoid those tiles with your key pieces or you may lose the piece quality you were counting on.
- **M3CH4GNU5 C4RL53N and Judit Polgeisha** mess with your setup and information, so do not assume the endgame you planned in staging is the one you get. Rebuild the plan once the board is clear.
- **Botezarro** turns same-colored squares into hazards when captures happen there, so choose your capture squares in the endgame carefully.
- **Mighty Kasparov** is the checkpoint boss and can show up at any boss checkpoint, so always keep your endgame fundamentals ready rather than building a plan that only works against one opponent.

## Tip 10: The Checklist Before You Commit

When the board is down to the final few pieces, run this before your last sequence:

- [ ] Have I set aside every Elite and boss-protected piece from my target list?
- [ ] Is the Crumble counter running, and for which side?
- [ ] Is the Stalemate Counter at 2/3 or higher?
- [ ] Does my next move take a piece, or force one next turn?
- [ ] Am I cutting escape squares instead of chasing?
- [ ] If I capture a Crumbler here, does the collapse hurt me?
- [ ] Does my final capture actually end the game, and is my capturing piece safe?

If the answer to the middle questions is weak, do not commit. Build the net one piece deeper and take the win when it is safe.

{{< pro-tip >}}<strong>The endgame is won in staging, not in the last turn.</strong>

Almost every endgame I have won comfortably was set up before the board got small. The position of my pieces when the enemy hit one piece determined whether the last few turns were a mechanical finish or a scramble. When you are in the midgame, ask where your pieces need to be standing for the ending you expect, and put them there early. That is the real endgame skill.{{< /pro-tip >}}
