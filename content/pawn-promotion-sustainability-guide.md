## The Promotion Trap

{{< callout type="verdict" >}}<strong>PROMOTION-AUTOMATIC UPGRADE</strong>

  Promoting a pawn to a queen sounds good, until your pawn-keyed Gambits stop paying and you lose the economy engine that carried you through the run. Promotion is a strategic decision, not a default.{{< /callout >}}

{{< meta-rating grade="A" label="Core strategy reference for intermediate players. Combines well with other guides." >}}


{{< section-divider >}}

Promotion in Gambonanza works differently than standard chess. It's not just about getting a queen, it's about what you LOSE when a pawn stops being a pawn. Certain Gambits and economy bonuses only trigger on pawn moves.

{{< callout type="danger" >}}<strong>THE BIG TRAP</strong>

  The most common community complaint: "I promoted my last pawn to a queen and my Pawn Gambit stopped working." This is by design. Once a piece promotes, it's no longer a pawn-and any Gambit that says "on pawn capture" or "when a pawn moves" simply doesn't apply. Plan your promotion timing carefully.{{< /callout >}}

## The Pawn Economy Loop

{{< callout type="tip" >}}<strong>THE PAWN CYCLE</strong>

  Pawns generate gold through pawn-specific Gambits. The most sustainable loop: use pawn Gambits to earn gold, spend gold on more Gambits, and keep pawns on the board earning. If you promote, the loop breaks.{{< /callout >}}

<div class="split-col">
<div class="col">

### Maintained Loop (Pawns stay pawns)
- Squirrel's Gambit keeps paying every time you gain a pawn
- Bug Catcher's Gambit pays $2 on every pawn capture
- Economy keeps growing every turn
- You can afford Legendary Gambits without farming

</div>
<div class="col">

### Broken Loop (Promoted too early)
- Pawn Gambits stop triggering
- Gold income drops to shop visits only
- You need to farm stages to recover
- The Legendary Gambit you were saving for? Too late

</div>
</div>

{{< pro-tip >}}<strong>Pro tip:</strong> The safer promotion strategy is <strong>promote sparingly and spread it out</strong>. Keep your pawn economy intact while slowly building a stronger army. Promote one key pawn at a time instead of clearing the board of pawns at once.{{< /pro-tip >}}

## Gambit Synergies with Promotion

<div class="synergy-table" style="overflow-x:auto">

| Gambit | Works Before Promotion | Works After Promotion | Note |
|--------|----------------------|---------------------|------|
| Squirrel's Gambit |- |-| Only pays while pawns keep entering the board |
| Bug Catcher's Gambit |- |-| Needs pawn captures to pay |
| Race Flag's Gambit |- |-| Actually pays on promotion, so it survives |
| War Horse's Gambit |- |-| Triggers on promotion into a Knight |
| Jump's Gambit |- |-| Movement tool, independent of piece type |
| Throne's Gambit |- |-| Triggers on promotion, gives a King |

</div>

{{< callout type="danger" >}}<strong>NOT SURE?</strong>

  Check the Gambit description. If it says "when a Pawn..." or "on Pawn capture"-promoting will reduce or remove that effect. If the effect is tied to "piece" or "any capture"-promote freely.{{< /callout >}}

{{< section-divider >}}

{{< pro-tip >}}<strong> Long run mindset</strong><br>
  Games that go 30+ turns are won by players who kept their economy intact. The player who promotes 4 pawns by turn 20 might look strong, but the player who kept 2 pawns as pawns, invested in Squirrel's Gambit, and bought Bug Catcher's + Race Flag's is still earning every turn. Who wins? The income.{{< /pro-tip >}}
