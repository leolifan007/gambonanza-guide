Chaining 101

One Gambit per turn is fine. Three Gambits on one capture wins games.

A chain is not a script. It is a board you build so that one move satisfies several Gambit trigger conditions at once.

See the All Gambits Guide for the full list, and the Deterministic Gambits Guide for the difference between guaranteed and chance-based triggers.

How Chaining Really Works

Every Gambit fires from a trigger condition. You do not “play” three Gambits in a row. Instead, you arrange the board so one action triggers several at once.

Trigger typeWhat sets it offExample Gambit
CaptureTaking an enemy pieceSilver Fork’s, Beth’s
LandingPlacing a piece from StockSkydiver’s, Spy’s, Dungeon’s
PromotionTurning a pawn into a queenRace Flag’s
Pawn actionA pawn moving or capturingSquirrel’s, Bug Catcher’s
Turn conditionThe enemy turn being skipped or alteredThunder’s

A chain happens when a single move satisfies more than one of these at the same time.

Pro tip:
Stack triggers, do not script them
The best chain setup is a board where your capturing piece also lands on a Gold Tile and completes a promotion route. One move, three payoffs. Build for overlap, not for a fixed sequence.

Chain Patterns That Actually Work

Capture Plus Tempo S

Thunder’s (a pawn capture skips the enemy turn) plus any capture Gambit. You take a piece, you keep the initiative, and the second Gambit fires on the same capture. This is the most reliable chain in the game and the reason Thunder’s is a top pick.

Landing Plus Payout A

Skydiver’s or Dungeon’s plus a Gold Tile landing. You spend one turn to land a piece, and the landing both repositions you and pays out. Best when you were going to land anyway.

Promotion Engine A

Race Flag’s around a pawn promotion. Push a pawn up a promotion route with support, and the promotion triggers both the new queen and the Gambit payout. Strong on the cramped opening board where a pawn can promote fast.

Pawn Economy Loop B

Squirrel’s plus Bug Catcher’s plus Race Flag’s. This is not a turn loop, it is an economy rotation: cheap pawn effects that pay out across the stage. See the Pawn Economy Loop for the full plan.

Timing Cheat Sheet

SituationBest chain
Enemy piece exposed, you need tempoCapture plus Thunder’s
You must land a piece anywaySkydiver’s or Dungeon’s onto a Gold Tile
Pawn is one step from promotingRace Flag’s plus support
Board is cramped at the startPawn economy rotation
Boss modifier limits your StockLand your key pieces before the fight

Rule: a chain you can pull off now beats a perfect chain that never lines up. If the overlap is not there, just take the best single action.

Building a Chaining Board

SlotWhat to look for
CoreOne trigger your board hits every game (Thunder’s is the default)
OverlapA second trigger that fires on the same move as your core
FlexAn answer for the next boss, swapped from the preview

Remember the 5-Gambit hold cap. Every slot is precious, so a chaining setup wants two Gambits on the same trigger rather than four that never co-fire.

Common Mistakes

MistakeFix
Buying Gambits with triggers your board cannot hitMatch the trigger to your pieces first
Hoarding Gambits that never co-fireDrop the one that does not overlap
Chaining against Mighty KasparovHe skips your next turn on a capture, so keep single-safe plays ready
Ignoring the landing costLanding costs a full turn, so make that turn earn multiple payoffs
Forgetting the 5-Gambit capSell dead weight before the shop
