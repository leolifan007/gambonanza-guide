Gambonanza Beginner Guide - Your First Clear in 5 Runs

What to do in your first five runs: which pieces to keep, what to buy in the first shop, when to restart, and the habits that stop new players losing winnable boards.

Stop Playing Chess. Start Playing Gambonanza.

THE ONE THING TO UNLEARN

If you came in hoping chess skill would carry you, you are about to be surprised. Gambonanza uses chess pieces, but it is a roguelike. There is no checkmate. You win a game by capturing every enemy piece. Play it like a tempo game, not a positional one.

That single reframe fixes most beginner mistakes. Here is what actually matters.

Pro tip:
Why chess players struggle early
Chess habits (protect every piece, build slow positions, wait for the opponent’s error) fight the game’s clock. Gambonanza rewards taking pieces and keeping the initiative, because both Crumble and the Stalemate Counter punish slow games.

The 60-Second Crash Course

ConceptWhat it actually is

WinningCapture every enemy piece. No checkmate

You play 5 stages of 5 games. The 5th game of each stage is a boss, and the board grows by a row after each stage. That is the whole loop. The Complete Walkthrough maps it start to finish.

Your First Couple of Runs

Do not read a guide before your first run. Just play one and see what happens.

You will probably win a game or two, then get surprised by something. That is the point. Watch for four things:

  1. How small the opening board is. Every move affects a lot of squares.
  2. That Gambits fire automatically. You do not “activate” them, they trigger from conditions.
  3. That the shop matters. Many new players forget to visit it. Read the boss preview in the lower-left every time.
  4. That both clocks exist. Crumble shrinks the board and the Stalemate Counter ends games, so tempo is everything.

After a run or two, the rest of this guide will click.

The Winning Habits

1. Attack, do not posture

THE GOLDEN RULE

On the cramped opening board, waiting loses. Every turn you spend “positioning” is a turn the counters move against you. Take pieces when you can.

Convert your advantage into captures. A game that ends fast is a game where neither clock can hurt you.

2. Knights are your early friends

AOn a small board, the knight's jump covers a large share of the squares and threatens things the enemy cannot easily block. Knights are at their best in the early game.

Open with knights, develop them early, and press. As the board grows, expect them to lose some of that edge. See the Knight Rush Opener.

SituationWhat to do
You spun two knightsDevelop both before anything else
Enemy piece is undefendedTake it, tempo over caution
You have a capture Gambit readyTake the capture, let the Gambit fire

3. Promotion is a win condition

THE PROMOTION MINDSET

A pawn one step from the far side is a threat worth protecting. Promote it and you gain a queen, which is the strongest piece on the board and the spine of many builds.

Strategy: when a pawn is close to promoting, spend resources protecting the route. A promoted queen is worth more than the pieces you might trade for it.

DO:

  • Clear the promotion route before pushing
  • Support the pawn on its way up
  • Have a plan for the new queen’s first move

DON’T:

  • Push a pawn with no support
  • Forget the promoted piece is now a target
  • Promote if it exposes your whole board

Gambits to Look For First

When you hit your first shop, do not just buy the cheapest thing. Look for Gambits your board can trigger.

PriorityGambitWhy it is a strong early pick
SThunder’sA pawn capture skips the enemy turn, the safest tempo tool in the game
ASilver Fork’sFires on captures, so it works with what you already do
ASquirrel’sCheap pawn payout, keeps gold flowing early
BJump’sRepositioning, useful on a cramped board

AVOID TRAP PICKS

A flashy expensive Gambit you cannot trigger is worse than a cheap one you fire every game. Rarity is not power, fit is power.

A Reliable Early Plan

StepActionWhy it works
1Develop your knights toward the enemyImmediate pressure on a small board
2Take any free captureTempo and gold at the same time
3Push a pawn toward promotion with supportBuilds toward a queen
4Buy a Gambit your board triggersReliable value every game
5Win the game, then read the next boss previewPrep beats surprise

WHY THIS WORKS

Early AI opponents react to the threats you create, so a fast start keeps them on the back foot. You are not outplaying them positionally, you are taking pieces before they can stabilize.

Boss Prep

Your first boss arrives on the 5th game of the first stage.

YOU WILL PROBABLY LOSE THE FIRST FEW

That is normal. Bosses change a rule, and learning each one takes a try or two. The shop preview tells you which modifier is coming, so read it.

How to lose less badly

DO THIS:

  • Read the boss preview before the shop
  • Buy the tool that answers the modifier
  • Keep a gold buffer going in
  • Learn one boss at a time

AVOID THIS:

  • Going in blind
  • Spending your whole reserve on the last shop
  • Repeating a plan the preview already counters

Once you have seen a boss twice, you will know exactly what answers it. The Boss Battle Guide covers all 8.

The Fastest Ways to Lose

THE 5 FASTEST WAYS TO LOSE
  1. Playing it like chess. Positional patience loses to tempo here.
  2. Hoarding gold. A big number does not save you from a bad board. Spend on what you need.
  3. Treating all Gambits as equal. Fit matters more than price.
  4. Ignoring both clocks. Crumble and the Stalemate Counter end games, so keep tempo up.
  5. Fighting a boss with no prep. Read the preview, buy the answer.

Final Advice

Gambonanza is not a brutal game, it is an unforgiving one. The difference matters:

  • Brutal games demand perfect execution
  • Unforgiving games demand that you stop repeating the same mistake
SEvery run teaches you something. The player who wins consistently is not the one with the best Gambits, it is the one who stopped making the same error twice.
