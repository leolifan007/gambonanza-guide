Enhanced AI Mode in Gambonanza 1.4: Should You Turn It On?

Everything you need to know about Gambonanza v1.4.0's Enhanced AI Mode: how to enable it, what it changes about enemy play, the achievement tradeoff, and when it is worth using.

Advertisement
Enhanced AI Mode does exactly what it promises: your enemies start playing real chess instead of chaotic gambling. But the tradeoff is severe - achievements and extra rewards are disabled while it is on. Use it for practice runs, learning piece interactions, or a chill tactical session. Turn it off before any run where you want progress to count.

The Short Answer

Enhanced AI Mode is the headline feature of Gambonanza v1.4.0, and it finally answers a complaint that has existed since launch: enemy pieces sometimes play like they are trying to lose. Flip the toggle and the enemy stops making suicidal trades, stops blundering into forks, and stops walking onto crumbling tiles. It is a fundamentally more conventional chess experience.

The catch is bigger than most players expect. While Enhanced AI is active, achievements do not trigger and extra rewards do not count. That is not a small footnote - it means every minute you spend in this mode is time that does not move you toward achievement progress, reward unlocks, or any of the meta progression tied to them.

So should you turn it on? Ask yourself one question: what do you want from this run? If you are grinding achievements, farming extra rewards, or chasing the intended roguelike chaos, keep it off. If you want to practice, learn piece interactions at your own pace, or play a cleaner game of chess, it is the best addition in the patch. For most players, the honest recommendation is: treat it as a training gym, and switch it off when you step into the ring for real runs.

How to Enable It

Three clicks: Main Menu -> Settings -> Extras. The toggle lives in the new Extra settings category introduced in v1.4.0, alongside other QoL additions like Unlock All and Erase Save Data. If you are on an older build, note that hotfix 1.4.0e fixed an issue where the setting could be inaccessible, so update before you go looking for it.

Nothing else changes. Your Gambits, your pieces, your economy - all identical. The mode is purely an enemy behavior switch, which is both its strength and its limit.

The Caveat, Front and Center

While Enhanced AI Mode is on, the game disables achievements and extra rewards. The developers did this deliberately: the mode alters the intended roguelike balance, so the game does not let you earn meta progress under altered conditions. If you are one achievement away from a milestone, do not finish that run in this mode.

This changes how you should schedule your sessions. A sensible pattern: warm up with one Enhanced AI run to learn a new Gambit or test a build concept, then restart and run the same concept on normal AI for actual progress. You get the practice benefit without wasting a run.

When to Turn It On

  • Chess purists. If you came to Gambonanza for the chess and tolerate the roguelike wrapper, this is the mode you have been waiting for.
  • Practice runs. New Gambit you do not understand? Test it here, where the enemy will not punish every mistake.
  • Learning piece interactions. The slower, more predictable enemy gives you room to experiment with promotion chains, tile control, and Gambit triggers.
  • Survival build testing. Because Crumble triggers later and the AI avoids crumble tiles, this is a safe sandbox for measuring how long a defensive build can hold.
  • Accessibility. Players who want a calmer pace or reduced cognitive load get a genuinely gentler experience.

When to Leave It Off

  • Achievement grinding. Any achievement run must happen on normal AI, no exceptions.
  • Extra reward farming. Rewards do not count, so farming runs in this mode are pure waste.
  • The intended experience. Gambonanza’s identity is chaos. The AI’s recklessness is part of the roguelike design - the same randomness that kills you can also hand you wins.
  • ELITE modifier content. The modifier is disabled entirely, so you cannot practice it here anyway.

How It Changes Crumble Pacing

The two Crumble changes matter more than they look. First, the AI actively avoids crumble tiles, which means your pieces survive longer - the enemy is not deliberately walking into a collapsing column to snipe your back line. Second, Crumble Mode triggers later in the match, giving you more turns to build your economy and board before the floor starts disappearing.

For survival builds, this is a meaningful upgrade to your testing environment: you see how your defense holds under pressure, but with a longer setup window. Just be careful about training bad habits. If you practice exclusively in Enhanced AI, normal runs will feel faster and harsher than you remember, because Crumble arrives earlier and the enemy does not care about falling tiles. Use the mode to learn the shape of a survival build, then validate it in normal mode before trusting it.

Enhanced AI Mode is the best training tool Gambonanza has ever shipped, and a genuinely nice accessibility option. It is not a replacement for normal mode, and the developers were right to disable achievements and rewards while it is active. Flip it on when you want to get better. Flip it off when you want to get rewarded.

Does Turning Enhanced AI Off Restore Achievements and Bonuses?

Yes. The Enhanced AI toggle is a live setting in Settings, under Extras. The patch notes state that enabling it disables achievements and extra rewards for that run. The moment you switch it back off, the next run is once again eligible for achievements and bonuses.

What this means in practice: you are not locking yourself out permanently. Players who want the traditional chess feel for one session, then go for 100 percent achievements on another, can flip the switch without losing progress. Just do not expect both at the same time on a single run.
